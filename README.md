# The Emperor's Problem: Ise, the Great Buddha, and the Making of Authority

make the choices that turn Ise's ritual renewal and Shōmu's Great Buddha into visible claims to rule

Online: open `index.html` (no install, no server) or scan `qr.png`.
Paper: not required — the exercise is interactive by design, and the
"show every answer" panel at the end doubles as a review sheet.

## What this is for

- **Audience**: students who have read Coaldrake's 'Ise Jingu' and Shōmu's proclamation; the exercise puts them in Shōmu's seat and asks them to make the decisions the readings explain
- **Time**: about 13 minutes
- **You will**: make the choices that turn Ise's ritual renewal and Shōmu's Great Buddha into visible claims to rule

## How it works

Answer a step, get told *why* the answer is right or wrong, then move
on. Nothing is scored; you are asked for a first attempt so you can see
which steps are shaky, and the ones you miss come back at the end.

## Sources

William H. Coaldrake, 'Ise Jingu,' in Architecture and Authority in Japan (Routledge, 1996), pp. 261–274; 'Proclamation of the Emperor Shōmu on the Erection of the Great Buddha Image,' in Sources of Japanese Tradition, vol. 1 (Columbia University Press, 1958), pp. 275–277.

## Caveats

Written material drifts: check anything load-bearing against the
sources above. Answers are in the page itself, so this is practice, not
assessment — that is deliberate.
